Two Tracks, One Vehicle

The same MUVMI tuk-tuk had two separate problems, so the team ran two engineering tracks in parallel: a software/control track and a hardware track. As team lead I focused personally on the software side and pulled both tracks together into one weekly deliverable.

Track 1 · my focus
EV Software · Regenerative Brake

Redesigned how the brake is sensed and controlled so the vehicle recovers energy smoothly while braking: an analog angular-position signal, speed-banded regen functions, and a vehicle-dynamics safety check, tested live on the vehicle.

Track 2 · integrated by me
Hardware · Driver Ergonomics

Redesigned and installed the physical parts: a new brake lever, a rest pedal, and the sensor mounting bracket. My teammates owned the build; I consolidated it into the combined design and the weekly submission.

The Problem

Information was gathered from MUVMI staff, MUVMI drivers, and literature research, and pointed to two separate problems on the same vehicle.

Two problems, one vehicle

1. Regenerative brake (software / control). The existing system used a pressure sensor producing a digital / step signal, and regeneration only triggered on accelerator release. The result was an uncomfortable braking feel and low energy-recovery efficiency.

2. Driver ergonomics (hardware). The brake pedal sat too steep, uncomfortable across an all-day shift. Measured joint angles fell short of the preferred values: knee 100° (preferred 110°) and ankle 75° (preferred 85°). Preferred angles were referenced from a published anthropometry source.

Software Track · Regenerative Brake

This was my personal focus, co-owned with one teammate. We replaced the pressure sensor with an analog angular-position sensor that reads the brake-lever angle, producing a smooth analog signal instead of a digital step, so regeneration can respond continuously to how hard the driver brakes.

Architecture choice. A Parallel regeneration scheme was chosen over Serial through an evaluation table:

ArchitectureEfficiency (of input power)Trade-offsDecision
Parallel~0.09 – 0.18Simpler mechanism, shorter braking duration, adaptable to the current design, cheaper✓ Chosen
Serial~0.15 – 0.30Higher efficiency, but complex, expensive, needs a new designNot chosen

Speed-banded regen functions. The control logic bands the regeneration response by vehicle speed, each band offering a Comfort and an Efficiency mode:

Speed bandRegen functionModes
0 – 15 km/hStepComfort / Efficiency
15 – 35 km/hCurveComfort / Efficiency
35 – 50 km/hRampComfort / Efficiency

To keep braking safe, we built a vehicle-dynamics model and a braking-ratio analysis of front/rear wheel-lock limits. We then built a physical prototype and tested it live on the vehicle at the I'm Park underground lot (Suan Luang), logging battery voltage from real regeneration on site to decide which functional graph and technique recovered the most energy, and to derive a calibration function for the angular sensor.

Hardware Track · Driver Ergonomics

My teammates owned the physical build; I integrated it into the combined design and the weekly submission. The team redesigned the brake lever to reduce the pedal angle and added a rest pedal giving the foot a resting position. Multiple design alternatives were screened pass/fail before the final design.
