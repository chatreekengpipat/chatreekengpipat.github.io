MUVMI Regenerative Brake
& Driver Ergonomics Design

A 5-person Mechanical Engineering design project at Chulalongkorn University, carried out in collaboration with MUVMI, an electric tuk-tuk ride-sharing service in Bangkok. The team tackled two threads on the same vehicle: an all-day brake pedal that sat too steep for comfortable driving, and a regenerative-brake system whose pressure sensor produced a coarse digital signal and only recovered energy on accelerator release. Final presentation 12 March 2020.

The Problem

Information was gathered from MUVMI staff, MUVMI drivers, and literature research, and pointed to two separate problems on the same vehicle.

Two problems, one vehicle

1. Driver ergonomics. The brake pedal sat too steep, uncomfortable across an all-day shift. Measured joint angles fell short of the preferred values: knee 100° (preferred 110°) and ankle 75° (preferred 85°). Preferred angles were referenced from a published anthropometry source.

2. Regenerative brake. The existing system used a pressure sensor producing a digital / step signal, and regeneration only triggered on accelerator release. The result was an uncomfortable braking feel and low energy-recovery efficiency.

Solution A · Ergonomics

The team redesigned the brake lever to reduce the pedal angle and added a rest pedal giving the foot a resting position. Multiple design alternatives were screened pass/fail before settling on the final design.

What the drivers reported

Lighter and easier braking · the brake was easier to reach · less foot fatigue · and a usable resting position while waiting.

Cost · Brake lever 180 ฿ + Pedal 95 ฿ + Rest pad 170 ฿ = 445 ฿ total

Solution B · Regenerative Brake

The pressure sensor was replaced with an analog angular-position sensor that reads the brake-lever angle, producing a smooth analog signal instead of a digital step. A sensor mounting bracket was designed, again screening multiple alternatives before the final mount.

[TODO: image]Sensor-mount CAD render / on-vehicle prototype photo

Architecture choice. A Parallel regeneration scheme was chosen over Serial through an evaluation table:

ArchitectureEfficiency (of input power)Trade-offsDecision
Parallel~0.09 – 0.18Simpler mechanism, shorter braking duration, adaptable to the current design, cheaper✓ Chosen
Serial~0.15 – 0.30Higher efficiency, but complex, expensive, needs a new designNot chosen

Speed-banded regen functions. The regeneration response was banded by vehicle speed, each band offering a Comfort and an Efficiency mode:

Speed bandRegen functionModes
0 – 15 km/hStepComfort / Efficiency
15 – 35 km/hCurveComfort / Efficiency
35 – 50 km/hRampComfort / Efficiency

To keep braking safe, the team built a vehicle-dynamics model and a braking-ratio analysis of front/rear wheel-lock limits. A physical prototype was built and tested on the vehicle, and a calibration function was derived for the angular sensor.
